What the agenda is telling you

Three things worth knowing before the trip is planned around the wrong part of it.

There is no slot to apply for.

The whole of Thursday is published as a single general-session block with no titles and no speakers. Whatever speaking exists is arranged directly with the people who invited you, and that ask has to be made now rather than in October.

Four open windows, and they are the event.

Two receptions, a breakfast and a lunch. Everything that matters here happens standing up, which means the preparation is conversation, not a talk.

Day one before five is their own people.

Lunch and the breakouts are marked internal. Arriving for the reception loses nothing.

What to say, and what to ask

One line, not two. In a bar nobody hears the second half of a paragraph.

If someone asks what Juno does

We go into the towns where these facilities get built and find out what actually changed — on the record, every claim sourced. Companies use it when somebody asks them to prove the community benefit and the usual answer does not hold.

Then stop talking and ask these

Three questions that get a data-center person talking about the exact problem this work solves, without pitching anything.

“When a community asks what the facility has actually done for them, who answers that, and with what?”

Almost nobody has a clean answer. The pause is the opening.

“Where do the numbers in that answer come from?”

Modelled, usually, and they know it. This is the claim-validation conversation without having to sell anything.

“Has anyone on your side actually spent time in the town?”

Normally no, and admitting it out loud costs them nothing in front of you.
