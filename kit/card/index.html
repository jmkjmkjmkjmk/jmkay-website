Open with this

Ask it, then stop talking. The answer tells you which of the eight lines below the conversation is about.

You are going to be asked what your facility has actually done for this town — by a reporter, a county board, or your own chief executive. Can you answer that today with something that holds up?

The thirty seconds

Two versions, for the two rooms that come up. The category is community evidence — the discipline between corporate communications, which asserts, and community engagement, which convenes.

Default

A room you are trusted in

The thing nobody budgets for when they build in a small town is listening. That is where we start — hours with the people living the change, the mayor and the man who runs the diner alike. Then every claim that comes out of it goes through a register with a source and a verdict attached, so what you publish holds when somebody hostile reads it.

Formal

A sceptical room, or one with procurement in it

When a company builds something big in a small place, the story gets told with or without them, and the loudest version is rarely the truest. We field long-form interviews in the market, verify every claim against a sourced record, and hand back an evidence base your own team can publish from. Negatives are counted. That is what makes it defensible.

The eight lines

Say the hook, not the name. The hook is what a buyer recognises as their own problem; the name is what goes on the invoice.

1

Market Impact Assessment

“You are about to be asked what your facility has done for this town, and today you do not have an answer that holds.”

A sourced evidence base: every claim registered and verdicted, the friction map, and the honest picture. Weeks rather than quarters.

2

Story & Champion Mapping

“Your best story is being told badly, by you. The people the community would actually believe have not been asked.”

The story inventory and a consented champion map, plus the stakeholder universe — who the story touches, from officials to your own site staff.

3

Community Storytelling Program

“You have messaging. You do not have stories anyone carries.”

Flagship film, champion profiles, short-form, photography and the activation toolkit — every asset traceable to the evidence base.

4

The Halo Index

“Your investment number means nothing to the average citizen. What did the place actually get?”

A banded, annually re-scored measure of what a facility gives back, across five dimensions. Methodology published, negatives counted.

5

Community Intelligence

“Every time someone leaves your team, your market knowledge leaves with them.”

The registers, the stakeholder map and the champion record as a living system — institutional memory that survives turnover.

6

The Playbook & Certified Researchers

“You want this everywhere you operate and you cannot clone the team that did the first three.”

The method as it is actually run and kept current, plus researchers trained against it: the same standard everywhere, verified by robust gates.

7

The Program Portal

“Your team is halfway around the world and you have no idea what is happening.”

One always-current place: schedule, deliverables, assets with thumbnails, transcripts. Nobody has to ask.

8

Claim Validation

“Your own materials already say ‘unvalidated’ in the footnote. Anyone hostile reads that footnote before they read your investment number.”

The claims you already publish, taken one at a time and verdicted against the record — the ones that hold with their sources attached, the ones that do not, flagged.

When they push back

Six that come up every time. The answer is short on purpose — a long answer reads as a defence.

“We already have an agency.”

Your agency tells the story. This verifies it first, then hands the evidence base over for them to activate. It makes their work stronger and it makes it defensible.

Do not

Do not position against the incumbent. The evidence base is upstream of what they do.

“How is this different from a survey?”

Surveys ask opinions. This audits reality — sourced metrics, on-the-record interviews, claims carrying verdicts. It is closer to diligence than to research.

Do not

Do not say “more rigorous”. Name the instrument: a register with rows anyone can audit.

“Why should anyone trust your number?”

Because the register shows its work. Every input sourced, every verdict recorded, negatives included and visibly lowering the score. Methodology published.

Do not

Do not reach for the word independent. The claim is the published method, not a status.

“Are you not just doing PR with extra steps?”

Public relations starts from what the company wants said. This starts from what the town actually says, and only what survives verification is published.

Do not

Do not get defensive. The distinction is the sale.

“How much of this is AI?”

It does the synthesis and the systems work — transcription, cross-referencing, holding every market to one standard. It never generates a quote and it never sources a claim.

Do not

Do not price it as hours. Hours invite “the machine did it, why am I paying”.

“What if the evidence comes back bad?”

Then you know before your critics publish it, with a map of exactly where the friction is and what would change it.

Do not

Do not soften this. A validation nobody can fail is a press release, and buyers know it.

Commit, or come back

The left column can be said in the room without checking with anyone. The right column gets one sentence — “let me come back to you on that” — and a call afterwards.

Commit to this in the room
  • That a first market gets scoped and into the field in weeks rather than quarters.
  • That every published claim carries its source and its verdict.
  • That negatives are in scope and get counted.
  • That interviews are long-form, on the record and consented.
  • That the standard is the same in every market, because the standards function runs centrally rather than market by market.
  • That the incumbent agency keeps the activation work.
  • That a discovery conversation costs them nothing.
Come back on this
  • Any price, rate, budget range or commercial structure.There is one answer and it is not yours to give in the room.
  • Any date, or any duration in weeks.A number said out loud becomes a commitment. Scoping sets it.
  • Who the flagship client is.“A global technology company” carries every room, and a screenshot forwards.
  • Anything that puts the category claim in writing.Say it in a room. Never in an email, until clearance and a second logo exist.
  • Scoring a market for a client who also wants us to publish for them.The two cannot sit in one contract. There is a regulatory clock on it.
  • A named reference, or a case study with a logo on it.Nothing is cleared for naming yet.

Words that travel, words that do not

Anything said in a room reaches an email by the end of the week. These are the phrases that survive the trip and the ones that do not.

Say
  • “A global technology company.”
  • “Further markets in motion.”
  • “Methodology published, negatives counted.”
  • “Proven instruments.”
  • “Eight validated, two flagged” — the claim audit, as a shape.
  • “Across the three markets.”
Do not say
  • Any count of further markets.The sanctioned phrase is “further markets in motion”.
  • Any rate, day rate or per-seat figure.Those live in one private file.
  • “Turnkey”, “product”, “plug and play”.The sanctioned phrase is “proven instruments”.
  • “Independent”, anywhere near the Halo Index.Use “methodology published, negatives counted”.
  • “Nobody else works this way” — in writing.Verbal only, in a room you can back it in.
  • The client's name on anything that could be forwarded.A screenshot forwards.
  • Any readiness percentage, and above all a zero.Say “not yet written down”.
  • “Storytelling” or “narrative”, in a New Mexico room.Those are liability words there. Use the validation framing.

After a good conversation

Four moves, and only the first happens in the room.

In the room

Offer a discovery conversation, not a proposal. It is the only ask.

Same day

Send the leave-behind. It is written to be forwarded internally.

Within the week

Hand the name over. The first-call sheet gets run against them.

Then

Scoping, which is where a price and a date come from.
