Sell-in cockpit

The talk track, what he pushes back on, who has seen what, and which of the ten things he asked for actually exist today — five of them, measured from disk rather than remembered.

THIS PAGE IS NOT SHARED. It carries candid reads on how a partner argues and a record of what has been said to whom.

a · The talk track

The twelve slides as spoken beats. Not a script — the one sentence that has to land on each. Slide five is the meeting: everything before it earns the right to open the board, everything after is detail.

b · The objection bank

Five he actually raises, each from the record rather than from imagination, plus the sixth thing, which is a risk rather than an objection.

His push-back has a fixed shape in the record: disarm, name a specific downstream risk — usually optics or how a client will read it — then defer to a named later checkpoint. Answering the stated objection without answering the deferral is how a yes becomes a maybe.

c · Who has seen what

A socialise tracker, not a distribution list. The order is load-bearing — one row is blocked on another and the reason is in the row.

d · The three questions, live

With their defaults, so a silence is visibly a decision rather than a gap.

e · What exists, checked

His ten, with the five that have a file test measured from disk right now. It goes stale the moment something gets built, so it is derived and never typed.
