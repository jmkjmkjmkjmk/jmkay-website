A Curated Resource

Anti-Racism in Tap Dance:
Films, Books & Performances

Tap dance is the original American jazz form — a vernacular art through which the history of race in America can be told. This collection gathers essential documentaries, feature films, books, and performances that illuminate the Black roots and legacy of tap.

Documentaries

Essential documentaries tracing the lineage, artistry, and revival of tap dance as a Black American art form.

1979No Maps on My TapsGeorge T. Nierenberg

No Maps on My Taps

1979 · Dir. George T. Nierenberg · 58 min
The spirit of tap in its heyday, shown through rare photos and Hollywood film clips. Three surviving hoofers — Sandman Sims, Chuck Green, and Bunny Briggs — tell the story of tap as an expression of Black heritage and culture. An indispensable historical record of this distinctly American art form.
Criterion Prime Video Plex (Free) Letterboxd

About Tap

1985 · Dir. George T. Nierenberg · 28 min
Introduced by Gregory Hines, this brilliant follow-up to No Maps on My Taps examines various styles of jazz tap through masters Steve Condos, Jimmy Slyde, and Chuck Green. Each dancer answers: "How does an artist discover their own individual style?"
Criterion Plex (Free) Letterboxd

Sammy Davis, Jr.: I've Gotta Be Me

2017 · Dir. Sam Pollard · 100 min · PBS American Masters
The first major film documentary to examine the performer's vast career and his journey for identity through the shifting tides of civil rights and racial progress during 20th-century America. A portrait of one of the most complex entertainers in American history.
PBS Prime Video Letterboxd
2006Been Rich All My LifeHeather Lyn MacDonald

Been Rich All My Life

2006 · Dir. Heather MacDonald · 79 min
An admiring portrait of the Silver Belles — a troupe of veteran Harlem tap dancers between 84 and 96 years old. Bertye Lou Wood, Cleo Hayes, Marion Coles, Fay Ray, and Elaine Ellis met in the 1930s as chorus dancers and reunited in 1985, still performing in matching glitter-encrusted costumes.

2004Plenty of Good Women DancersKodish & Dornfeld

Plenty of Good Women Dancers

2004 · Dir. Debora Kodish & Barry Dornfeld · 53 min
Features exceptional Philadelphia African American women tap dancers active from the 1920s–1950s. Restricted to few roles, often unnamed and uncredited, these women remained anonymous even within their own communities. Historic clips and vivid recollections paint a dynamic portrait of veteran women hoofers.

Feature Films

Narrative films that center tap dance and its intersection with race in America.

Stormy Weather

1943 · Dir. Andrew L. Stone · Starring Lena Horne & Bill Robinson
A landmark all-Black musical featuring Bill "Bojangles" Robinson, Lena Horne, Cab Calloway, Fats Waller, and the Nicholas Brothers. Their legendary "Jumpin' Jive" sequence is considered one of the greatest dance numbers ever committed to film.
YouTube (Free) Prime Video Letterboxd

The Cotton Club

1984 · Dir. Francis Ford Coppola
Coppola's lavish drama set in 1930s Harlem's legendary Cotton Club, where the best Black entertainers performed for white-only audiences. Features Gregory Hines alongside Richard Gere in parallel stories of musicians and gangsters navigating the racial divide.
Tubi (Free) Prime Video Letterboxd

Tap

1989 · Dir. Nick Castle · Starring Gregory Hines & Sammy Davis Jr.
Gregory Hines stars as an ex-con torn between crime and tap. Sammy Davis Jr.'s final film appearance. Features a legendary challenge-dance sequence with Arthur Duncan, Bunny Briggs, Steve Condos, Harold Nicholas, Jimmy Slyde, and other masters of the form.
Tubi (Free) Prime Video Letterboxd

Bamboozled

2000 · Dir. Spike Lee · Starring Damon Wayans & Savion Glover
Spike Lee's blistering satire about a Black TV writer who pitches a modern minstrel show — and it becomes a hit. Savion Glover stars as a tap dancer exploited by the entertainment industry, drawing direct lines between minstrelsy's history and modern media representation.
JustWatch Letterboxd

White Nights

1985 · Dir. Taylor Hackford · Starring Gregory Hines & Mikhail Baryshnikov
Gregory Hines plays an American tap dancer who defected to the Soviet Union, paired with Baryshnikov's Russian ballet dancer trying to escape. Their duets — tap meeting ballet — are explosive demonstrations of how dance transcends cultural and political boundaries.
Tubi (Free) Prime Video Letterboxd

Bojangles

2001 · Dir. Joseph Sargent · Starring Gregory Hines
Gregory Hines portrays the legendary Bill "Bojangles" Robinson in this biographical film tracing Robinson's rise from Richmond streets to Broadway stardom and Hollywood, navigating the brutal racial politics of early 20th century entertainment.
Tubi (Free) Prime Video Letterboxd
Section 02

Books & Reading

Essential reading on tap dance history, the Black roots of American vernacular dance, and the intersection of race and performance.

What the Eye Hears: A History of Tap Dancing

Brian Seibert · 2015
Winner of the Anisfield-Wolf Book Award. The definitive history — from tap's origins in the jig, clog dancing, and dances brought from Africa by enslaved people, through minstrelsy, Broadway, Hollywood, its post-war decline, and contemporary revival.
Amazon →

Brotherhood in Rhythm: The Jazz Tap Dancing of the Nicholas Brothers

Constance Valis Hill · 2000
The definitive account of Fayard and Harold Nicholas, whose acrobatic brilliance redefined tap. Traces their journey from Philadelphia vaudeville through Hollywood and their complex navigation of race in the entertainment industry.
Amazon →

Jazz Dance: The Story of American Vernacular Dance

Marshall & Jean Stearns · 1968
The seminal history whose final chapter was titled "The Dying Breed" — written just before the 1970s tap revival proved it beautifully wrong. Essential context for understanding every documentary on this list.
Amazon →

Tap! The Greatest Tap Dance Stars and Their Stories

Rusty E. Frank · 1990
An oral history built from first-person interviews with the dancers themselves — their own words about the golden age, the lean years, and the revival. An essential companion to the documentary record.
Amazon →

Digging the Africanist Presence in American Performance

Brenda Dixon Gottschild · 1996
Uncovers the African roots embedded in American concert dance, popular entertainment, and everyday movement. Essential for understanding how Black aesthetics shaped — and were often uncredited in — American dance forms including tap.
Amazon →

Waltzing in the Dark: African American Vaudeville and Race Politics in the Swing Era

Brenda Dixon Gottschild · 2000
Examines the world of African American vaudeville — the vibrant performance circuit that nurtured virtually every tap dancer on this list — and the racial politics that both constrained and propelled their artistry.
Amazon →

Steppin' on the Blues: The Visible Rhythms of African American Dance

Jacqui Malone · 1996
Explores the rhythmic foundations of African American dance forms, from ring shouts through jazz tap to contemporary hip-hop, revealing the continuous thread of African-derived movement aesthetics in American culture.
Amazon →

The Black Tradition in American Dance

Richard A. Long · 1989
A comprehensive visual and narrative survey of the Black dance tradition from African origins through modern concert dance, placing tap within the broader continuum of Black movement art in America.
Amazon →

The Royalty of Negro Vaudeville: The Whitman Sisters

Nadine George-Graves · 2000
Recovers the story of the Whitman Sisters — the highest-paid Black act on the TOBA circuit — and their negotiation of race, gender, and class in African American theater from 1900–1940. A crucial, under-told chapter in tap history.
Amazon →

Secret Daughter: A Mixed-Race Daughter and the Mother Who Gave Her Away

June Cross · 2006
The memoir that accompanies the Frontline documentary. June Cross tells the full story of her parents — a white actress and a Black vaudevillian — and growing up hidden across the racial divide of mid-century America.
Amazon →

Savion Glover's Big Break on PBS with Gregory Hines

At just 15, Savion Glover appeared alongside his mentor Gregory Hines on PBS's Great Performances special "Tap Dance in America." The moment a new generation of tap found its voice.
