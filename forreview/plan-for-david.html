The plan for David

Groundwork

Making the research method something a small team can run

Draft for David White · 9 September 2026 · Groundwork

Two pages, then the detail. Three questions are open at the end, each with what I would do and what happens if we do nothing. Everything else here is decided and I am not asking you to reopen it unless you want to.

1 · We replicate ourselves. Here is the part that does not replicate yet.

Your sentence, from the drive: we have to just replicate ourselves. You don't necessarily need to go to New Mexico — you just need to instruct somebody how to go to New Mexico and what questions to ask. And then the hard part, which you also named: the trick is synthesising it all. Is there somebody who can take a battery company and an Oracle and synthesise it? And have you quality control?

I went through everything we have built across the three markets and scored it by one test: could somebody we hire next month pick this up and use it, with nobody to call. The answer splits cleanly, and not where I expected.

Going to a market and getting the interviews is largely packaged. So is recording it, filing it, and closing a market out. The instruments exist, they are blank, and they work.

Turning verified evidence into a finding, and a finding into something a client reads, is not written down anywhere. Not partially — not at all. That is the honest answer to your question, and it is the thing this plan is mostly about. Everything else is packaging.

2 · The proof is the thing you already named

The recaps. Your words: the single best thing, circulating through the booth, advertising for Juno, and the reason the Adria calls stopped being contentious. That is the model for all of this — something that already ran, already changed somebody's temperature, then packaged so a second person can run it.

Second piece of proof, and I would rather show you than describe it: the morning dashboard you asked for on the drive is built. One place, every market, what moved. Ten minutes at the top of the meeting.

3 · Your list, handed back

Ten things you committed to on the drives on 20 August. I went back through the tapes rather than my memory, so the timecodes are there if you want to check I have not put words in your mouth. Five now exist.

  • Your morning dashboard. “I'd love to have this comprehensive dashboard I can go to in the morning.” Built.
  • The AI line for the Oracle proposal. “Before I go to Oracle, I will definitely make sure everything is in.” Named, scoped, and priced as a systems line rather than as hours — hours invite the machine did it, why am I paying.
  • Ask for the bigger number up front. “Once you ask for it to get approved, that's it.” Now a standing rule, with the contingency mechanism written beside it.
  • Who absorbs the surprises. “I'm not going to eat your surprise.” Written down, rather than living in one sentence on one recording.
  • Eight validated, two flagged. Your claim-audit idea is now a service line, because you were right that nobody else is offering it.
  • Speed as a priced deliverable — the thing DC Works taught you to name.
  • Site community managers, early. Not built. It belongs in the field kit and it is a half- day.
  • The stakeholder-category list you can hand a new client in a first meeting — the one where you guarantee there are divisions they had not thought of.
  • Researcher names, and how we find more. Section 5.
  • The educational repository. Noted, not started, and I would leave it until there is a bench to read it.

4 · What a new researcher gets on day one

The kit is real and most of it already exists — it needed extracting rather than inventing. Five blank instruments, the interview guide as a fill-in rather than as three markets' worth of notes, the evidence discipline, the citation grammar, the consent and compensation rules, and a reference set of finished work so they can see what good looks like before they try to produce it.

The one genuinely new build is a market scaffold — one command that stands up a new market's folders, its empty registers, and the checks, and tells you whether it is ready. Without it the method is repeatable in principle and hand-assembled in practice.

And the synthesis instruction, which does not exist and which I am writing. I would rather show you one worked example — evidence to finding to report section — than describe it. That is the piece the whole thing rests on and I am not going to claim it is done.

5 · The bench

Juno hires them. I certify them. That keeps you as the employer of record and the commercial relationship, and it keeps me on the standard rather than on payroll. It is the simplest version and I think it is the right one.

Recruit tier one regionally — local fluency beats travel budget, and it is better for the work. The profile is a field journalist with diligence discipline: comfort in a strange town, long-form non-leading interviewing, evidence hygiene, writing that survives a fact-check. Not an agency account person.

There is a one-page checklist of what a researcher has to be able to do before they run a market alone. It is in the appendix. It is a checklist, not a programme — you asked for names and a way to vet them, and that is what this is.

On the first hard market: it stays the two of us, with somebody in the periphery watching and learning. That was your read on the drive and I think it is right. A bench debuting publicly in the hardest room we have had is a bad first outing for something we want to sell.

6 · What it costs, and where the margin is

Attached is a per-market model rather than a rate card. The shape: a per-market platform fee that covers the instruments, the registers and the standards function; field hours billed as hours; and the central function — transcription, diarisation, the evidence machinery — run centrally and priced as part of the platform rather than donated as good practice.

It is run centrally on purpose. It is the only way the standard is the same in every market, and it is what the platform fee actually buys. A regionally-hired researcher will not run this on a laptop and should not have to.

The model shows the per-market cost against what a sourced research pass runs at today. The point of it is that your margin per market goes up, not down — if it does not, I have built the wrong thing and I would rather find that out over this page than in October.

Because you employ the bench, the delivery hours are yours. What I am pricing is the method, the instruments and the standards function — the things that make the hours worth more than the hours.

7 · The sequence

  • Now to the end of the month. The kit gets blanked and dated. Nothing needs you.
  • September is the field. Three trips, and I will shadow one candidate researcher under real conditions while I am already there. That is the certification pilot and it costs nothing extra.
  • Late September. The scaffold and one worked synthesis example get built.
  • Early October. One meeting. I will open with the dashboard on screen.

Nothing here needs a reply from you to keep moving. If you say nothing until October, all of it still happens.

8 · Three open questions

1. What is Juno's actual margin per market?

It is the one number I cannot derive and you can. It is not an audit and I do not need the workings — I need to know what the platform fee has to sit inside.

What I'd do. Give me a range rather than a number, and I will build the model against the bottom of it. If we don't decide. I price against my own estimate, and we find out in October whether it eats your margin — which is the version where you quietly stop using the apparatus and neither of us says so.

2. Which market does the bench debut in, and how far in?

My read is the first hard market stays the two of us with an apprentice watching. But you know which rooms are survivable better than I do.

What I'd do. Debut them somewhere forgiving, apprenticing, and keep the hard market ours. If we don't decide. We default to apprentice-only, which is the safe version and slower.

3. The name.

Groundwork is a working name. I have not seen a reaction to it and I would rather have one before it is on anything a client sees.

What I'd do. Leave it open until October and decide it with the positioning rather than before it. If we don't decide. It stays Groundwork by inertia, which is how names get decided badly.

9 · Where I want your redraft

You did this with the interview guide in July and it came back better. The section I most want your version of is section 6 — the economics — because you know what you can carry into a room and I am guessing at it.
